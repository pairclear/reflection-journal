## After a fight

**Use when:** A fight happened in the last hours or days, and it keeps going round in your head.

**First:** If you are still boiling, take a break first: at least 20 minutes of something else (a walk, a shower, music). Writing goes better once your body has calmed down.

1. **What happened, as a camera would have recorded it? Only words and actions, no interpretations.**  
   _What was the first moment it started going wrong?_
2. **What did I feel during the fight, and what do I feel now?**  
   _Which feeling is the hardest to admit?_
3. **What did I tell myself about my partner in that moment?**  
   _What else could be true? Name one other explanation._
4. **What did I need in that moment and not get?**  
   _Did I say it out loud, or did I expect it to be guessed?_
5. **What was my part in how it went? Not blame, just my share.**  
   _What would I do differently in that same moment?_
6. **What might my partner have felt or needed?**  
   _This is a guess. How could I check it instead of assuming?_
7. **What do I want now: to talk it through, to apologise, to ask for something, or to let it go?**  
   _What first sentence could I start with?_

## Before a hard conversation

**Use when:** You need to raise something difficult with your partner and want to go in clear-headed.

**First:** If you are afraid of how your partner will react, put your safety first. Preparing better words does not make an unsafe conversation safe. See the box at the bottom of the page.

1. **What do I want to talk about, in one or two sentences?**  
   _If the talk goes well, what will be different afterwards?_
2. **What exactly do I want to ask for? A concrete action, not "be more attentive".**  
   _What would a small first version of it look like?_
3. **What am I afraid of in this conversation?**  
   _If that happened, what would I do?_
4. **How might my partner see this situation?**  
   _What in their view do I agree with, even a little?_
5. **Write the opening line, starting from yourself: "I feel ... when ..., and I would like ..."**  
   _Read it aloud. Does it sound like a request or like a charge?_
6. **When and where is a good moment for it?**  
   _Not in passing, not when someone is tired or hungry, not in front of others._
7. **What will I do if the conversation starts going badly?**  
   _Which words will I use to ask for a pause, and when will we come back to it?_

## Weekly couple check-in

**Use when:** Once a week, alone or with your partner, to notice what is going on between you before it piles up.

**First:** If you do it together, each of you writes alone first, then you share only what you want to share. Start with the good parts.

1. **What was good between us this week?**  
   _What did my partner do that I appreciated, and did I tell them?_
2. **When did I feel close this week? When did I feel distant?**  
   _What was going on around those moments?_
3. **Is there something I left unsaid?**  
   _Is it worth bringing up, and how?_
4. **What do I need from us next week? One concrete request.**  
   _How will I ask for it?_
5. **What can I give next week? One small thing.**  
   _When exactly will I do it?_
6. **What are we looking forward to together?**  
   _If nothing comes to mind, what could we plan?_

## When I feel jealous

**Use when:** Jealousy has come up and you want to understand it before you act on it.

**First:** Jealousy is a feeling, not a fact about your partner. If jealousy in your relationship has turned into control or threats, from either side, that is a reason to talk to a specialist.

1. **What happened that set off the feeling? Facts only.**  
   _What did I see or hear myself, and what did I add in my head?_
2. **What exactly am I afraid of?**  
   _Losing them? Being replaced? Being deceived? Mattering less?_
3. **How strong is the feeling right now, from 0 to 10?**  
   _What makes it stronger, and what makes it weaker?_
4. **What did I do with it: check, ask, stay silent, accuse?**  
   _How did that work for us?_
5. **What would help me feel more secure in this relationship?**  
   _Is it something to ask for, something I can do myself, or both?_
6. **Do I want to say something to my partner about it?**  
   _How can I say it as my feeling, not as a charge against them?_

## When we feel distant

**Use when:** Things are not bad, but you feel further apart than before.

**First:** Distance is common in long relationships and usually builds slowly. The point here is to notice it, not to find who is to blame.

1. **When did I last feel really close to my partner? What was happening then?**  
   _What made that moment possible?_
2. **What has changed since then?**  
   _Between us, in the life around us, in me?_
3. **What do I miss most?**  
   _Is it something specific: time, touch, talking, laughing together?_
4. **What do I usually do when I feel distant: pull back, reach out, wait?**  
   _What does my partner see of this from the outside?_
5. **What small step towards contact could I take this week?**  
   _What would make it easy for my partner to respond?_
6. **Do I want to talk about this distance with my partner?**  
   _How could I start without blame?_

## After a breakup

**Use when:** A relationship has ended, recently or a while ago, and you want to sort out your thoughts.

**First:** Go at your own pace and skip any question that is too much today. If for weeks you can hardly sleep, eat or work, or you have thoughts of not wanting to live, please talk to a specialist or a helpline.

1. **What happened, in a few sentences?**  
   _Which version do I tell other people, and which do I tell myself?_
2. **What am I feeling most these days?**  
   _What helps, even a little?_
3. **What do I miss, and what do I not miss at all?**  
   _Both lists can be true at the same time._
4. **What did this relationship show me about what I need?**  
   _What do I not want to repeat?_
5. **Who and what supports me right now?**  
   _Who could I message today?_
6. **What do I want to do for myself this week?**  
   _Something small and doable counts._
